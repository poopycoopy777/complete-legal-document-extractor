Appeal and Response Briefs - Argument Sections

Argument sections for the twenty appealable orders in D:
\OrderBench\dataset\orders.jsonl, written as they would be ﬁled.
Each ﬁle is the argument section of a brief for one order: the caption and standard of review,
the issue presented, the argument with authority cited inline, and a conclusion stating the relief
sought. Statement of the case, statement of facts, tables of authorities, and the certiﬁcate pages
are outside the scope of these ﬁles, as is the rest of the brief.

16.1, 26, 30, 37 and its subdivisions, 38, 39, 40, 45, 52, 55.1, 56, 58, 59, 60, 81, 107 and 121;
C.R.M. 7; C.A.R. 4, 10 and 27; C.R.S. ss 13-5-301, 13-17-101 and 13-17-102, 13-20-1101,
13-22-301 through 13-22-307, 13-91-101 et seq., 14-10-116, 14-10-120, 14-10-122, 14-10-124,
14-10-129, 14-14-101 through 14-14-104, the open records provisions at C.R.S. ss 24-72-201
through 24-72-204, the criminal justice records provisions at C.R.S. ss 24-72-301 through
24-72-305, and the sealing provisions at C.R.S. ss 24-72-701 through 24-72-707; Colo. Const.
art. II, s 23; U.S. Const. amend. I.

Provenance

Every section was written from the procedural question each order resolves, as stated in the
prompt ﬁeld of the dataset, together with the case caption, court, and docket number. No
online search and no retrieval was used, and no external source was consulted. The citations
above are recalled from memory and stated as a ﬁling lawyer states them.
holding and reasoning_hooks in orders.jsonl, and the order texts under dataset/
text/, were deliberately not read. Those ﬁelds are the benchmark's answer key. An argument
section written after reading the ruling it responds to would not be an argument section at all,
and reading them would have defeated the purpose of the exercise. Where a section responds to
a ruling, it does so from the procedural posture the prompt describes.
